## 1. Evaporation

The sun heats water in oceans, lakes and rivers. Some of that water turns into vapour and rises into the air. Plants also release water vapour through their leaves, which is called *transpiration*.

### 1.1 Why it matters

Evaporation moves an enormous amount of water every day. Without it, there would be no clouds, no rain, and very little fresh water on land.

## 2. Condensation

As water vapour rises, it cools down. Cooler air cannot hold as much vapour, so tiny droplets form around specks of dust. Billions of these droplets together make a cloud.

- Warm air holds more water vapour.
- Cold air holds less.
- Dew on grass in the morning is condensation too.

## 3. Precipitation

When droplets in a cloud bump into each other, they join and grow heavier. Eventually they fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail, depending on the temperature.

> Fun fact: a single cloud can weigh more than a million kilograms.

## 4. Collection

Water that falls collects in oceans, rivers, lakes and underground stores called aquifers. From there, the cycle starts all over again.

| Stage | What happens | Driven by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evaporation | Liquid becomes vapour | The sun's heat |
| Condensation | Vapour becomes droplets | Cooling |
| Precipitation | Droplets fall | Gravity |
| Collection | Water gathers | Gravity and land shape |
